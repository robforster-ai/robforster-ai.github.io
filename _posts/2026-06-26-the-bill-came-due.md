A short memo, because I've now had this conversation with three CFOs in a month.

2026 is the year the finance function stopped nodding along and started asking where the return is. The honest answer, for most enterprises, is uncomfortable.

## The numbers

MIT's NANDA study landed the headline everyone quotes: [about 95% of generative AI pilots show no measurable return on the P&L](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/). You can argue with the exact figure. You can't argue with the direction.

The one that worries me more is quieter. S&P Global Market Intelligence found the share of organisations [abandoning most of their AI initiatives before production rose from 17% to 42% in a single year](https://www.ciodive.com/news/AI-project-fail-data-SPGlobal/742590/), with the average organisation scrapping close to half its proofs of concept. That's not a technology plateau. That's a portfolio of things that were never going to ship, being quietly written off.

## It hit where the org-chart was weakest

Here's what I keep telling people, and it isn't comfortable:

> The reckoning didn't hit the companies with the worst models. It hit the ones with the weakest owners.

The spend was real. GPUs were bought, licences were signed, consultancies were retained. What was missing was the unglamorous layer underneath — a named accountable owner, a production runbook written before the model, a measurement spine that went live before go-live. The money went to demos with no path to production, exactly as I wrote back in the spring. The bill for that arrived all at once.

## The good news

The fix is cheaper than the failure. It's org design, not more compute. The 5% who are showing a return did the boring things: they picked one use case a single team understood, wrote the ops runbook first, backfilled the model to it, and measured for ninety days before scaling anything.

If your AI programme is under budget review this quarter, don't lead with the model roadmap. Lead with the ownership map. That's the line item finance is actually asking about.
